▸ The full worked answers

Worked answers

1. Shared passwords

RowsAccountsHow you can tell
1, 3nora, priyaidentical SHA-256 hashes: no salt, so the same password always gives the same hash
4, 6quinn, samidentical scrypt hashes, because both rows use the same salt
5rosaalso has quinn's password, and nobody can see it: her own salt makes her hash different. That is what a salt is for. (We know because we made the data.)

2. Stored safely?

RowVerdictWhy
1–3not safeplain SHA-256 is fast (1,554,687 guesses a second on one core, 21.2 billion on one GPU) and unsalted, so equal passwords show up as equal hashes and one guess tests every row at once
4, 6slow hash, broken saltscrypt is right, but the salt is reused: the two rows leak that they match. Give every user a fresh random salt (secrets.token_bytes(16))
5safescrypt + its own random salt: the way the video recommends

3. Five random words

StepArithmeticResult
all passphrases7,776⁵2.84 × 10¹⁹
on average, half2.84 × 10¹⁹ ÷ 21.42 × 10¹⁹ guesses
plain SHA-256, one core1.42 × 10¹⁹ ÷ 1,554,687 / s290 thousand years
scrypt, one core1.42 × 10¹⁹ ÷ 24.5 / s18 billion years: longer than the universe has existed (13.8 billion)

Even with plain SHA-256 on one RTX 4090 it is 21 years (the video's number). The words do most of the work; scrypt multiplies it again, about 63,457 times.
